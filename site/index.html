      How this works
      1. Discover. Each day, collect every article in English Wikipedia's daily top 1,000 over the last 30 days.
      2. Classify. Use Wikidata to keep only creative works (films, TV, songs, albums, games, books, comics) released at least 10 years ago.
      3. Measure. Compare each work's average daily views over the last 14 days with its median over the previous ~11 months. Works at 3× or more are revivals.
      4. Explain. Search Wikidata near the start of the spike for a death of someone credited, a sequel, adaptation, remake or new entry in the series (released or upcoming), a new work by the same creator, or a round-number anniversary.
      5. Connect. Group spikes by the event they share, and group unexplained spikes by a person they share when they started rising at the same time.
      6. Filter. Set aside seasonal items that were also elevated this time last year, and spikes that are almost all desktop traffic (real surges are mostly mobile).

      Read with care. These are leads, not conclusions. Wikidata is edited by volunteers and can be incomplete or wrong. Memes, TV airings and ad placements aren't recorded there, so "No trigger found" means "worth investigating", not "no cause". Two-work threads can be coincidence.
